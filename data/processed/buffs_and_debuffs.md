# Buffs and Debuffs

Buffs and debuffs are core gameplay mechanics in Bee Swarm Simulator. Players can obtain them from various sources, including amulets, quest givers, items, ability tokens, codes, and different dispensers. Some effects are exclusive to a single source (e.g., the Splinter Trap only comes from the Stick Bug Challenge), while others can be obtained through multiple methods (e.g., blue boosts can come from the Blueberry Dispenser, bees, or flowers).

All status effects are displayed in the top margin of the screen, above the main menu. They are sorted from left to right, with newer effects appearing on the right. Hovering over an icon provides information regarding stack amount, effect details, and duration (if applicable). On certain devices, accessory icons may appear here instead of next to the hotbar.

## Trivia

*   Puffshroom Blessing, Festive Nymph Blessing, and Robo Party Blessing are currently buggy; they stop working after rejoining a server even if displayed above the player. A manual refresh is required to keep them applied.
*   The icon for the Frozen debuff originates from Ice Cube, a character in Battle for Dream Island.
*   The 3YearParty code (now expired) previously granted unlimited gumdrops for an hour, but it had a glitch where the buff disappeared if the player left and rejoined; Onett subsequently removed the buff from the code.
*   The shortest recorded buff is Coconut Haste Surge (2 seconds), while the longest are Honeyday Event, Launch Code Boost, and Galentine's Blessing (48 hours each).
*   Speaking to Onett during Beesmas grants the player the Honeyday Event buff, even if the hive has not been claimed yet.
*   The Splinter Trap icon is identical to the Haste+ icon, which is ironic given their opposing effects.
*   Receiving more stacks of Robo Bear Challenge debuff is less detrimental; for instance, in round 2, the capacity debuff is x0.12, which is better than x0.1 in round 1.
*   Haste+ was originally intended to be one of Ninja Bee's abilities but was later scrapped.
